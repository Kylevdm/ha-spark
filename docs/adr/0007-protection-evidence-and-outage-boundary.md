# Protection evidence and the Home Assistant outage boundary

Status: accepted (2026-10-12), confirmed by the owner in [Battery-to-car protection: stale evidence and whole-HA outage behavior](https://github.com/Kylevdm/ha-spark/issues/267). This is a v1 planning contract, not implemented behavior. It refines the protection and fallback policy in [ADR-0005](0005-integration-authority-and-operating-modes.md) and the earlier unreadable-EV rule from [Battery feeds the car when the EV charges without a matching Octopus dispatch](https://github.com/Kylevdm/ha-spark/issues/170).

The integration owns protection while Home Assistant runs, including when the add-on is unavailable. Evidence must be fresh under a validated, source-specific maximum age; an unchanged cached HA state is not proof of a new report. Unknown EV or dispatch evidence starts no new hold. An already confirmed EV hold survives unreadable or stale evidence, add-on restarts, and integration restarts until five fresh consecutive not-charging reports or an explicit owner handover that disables protection. A known dispatch hold lasts only until its last trusted end time; stale updates cannot extend it. Fresh EV evidence or a retained EV hold may continue protection after that deadline. A retained hold that cannot clear raises a degraded alert and requires explicit owner action to release without clear evidence.

During an add-on outage, an applicable hold outranks the integration's fixed fallback charging. The overnight exception applies only inside a verified battery charge window **and** when fresh battery-direction evidence shows no discharge into the car. Missing or discharging power evidence applies protection. Invalid SoC blocks charging, export, and any SoC-dependent protection command; a separately validated, read-back-verified discharge-only hold may continue if its command needs no SoC value. Neither the integration nor the add-on invents a substitute SoC.

At hold release, the integration checks current authority, fresh evidence, and the intended schedule and limits, then verifies restoration before removing the hold. Optimise uses a fresh plan; Protect restores the manual charging schedule. During temporary manual control, only protection-owned controls may be restored: the integration must not overwrite the owner's other edits. A failed restoration or read-back leaves the hold in place and reports the fault. On startup, the integration reads durable ownership and last confirmed hold state, current EV and dispatch evidence, and device state before releasing a hold or applying fallback or plan writes. It renews an expiring device hold only after validating its command and authority. A deliberate HA shutdown preserves a verified active hold unless the owner explicitly takes control; shutdown is not evidence that the EV stopped.

These rules promise protection only while HA and the integration run. Graceful shutdown can leave a device control in place, but an abrupt HA outage cannot execute cleanup or renewal. No Solis switch persistence, AlphaESS dead-man behavior, or gateway watchdog is assumed without a model-specific test. The setup UI must disclose the remaining whole-HA outage risk. Protect and Optimise are unavailable for a model until a hold command, its read-back, release, side effects, and freshness-capable evidence have been validated; Observe remains available. A hybrid model whose coarse switch stops solar requires a validated discharge-only control. Unattended battery export is prohibited where a resident export window repeats daily unless a validated device-resident expiry or equivalent bound prevents export after HA stops. A repeating charge window may serve as the agreed fallback.

## Required validation before implementation

- Establish the actual report cadence and trustworthy freshness signal for each supported EV, dispatch, and battery-direction source, then set and test its maximum age. Test stale positive, stale negative, unavailable, and recovery states separately.
- On each supported Solis model, verify hold and release read-back, whether the switch survives HA stop, HA restart and device restart, and whether it also stops PV. Verify how timed export windows behave without HA and whether any device-resident expiry can bound them. Do not generalise the battery-only AC-coupled result to a hybrid model.
- On each supported AlphaESS model, test the local dispatch hold under real house load, its PV and charging side effects, duration units and expiry without renewal, restart behavior, read-back, SoC field requirements, and precedence against resident charge or discharge schedules. [AlphaESS can't enforce holds: no hold surface](https://github.com/Kylevdm/ha-spark/issues/196) tracks the reference-hardware test.
- Test a graceful stop, abrupt HA loss, add-on loss, and restart while an EV hold is active. Confirm that no startup or shutdown path enables battery discharge into a still-charging car; the policy for [Clean shutdown releases a live EV charging hold](https://github.com/Kylevdm/ha-spark/issues/195) is settled here, while its implementation remains separate.
